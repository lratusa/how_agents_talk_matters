# Related Work — Response-Conditioned Review Topology for LLM Reasoning

Literature cutoff 2026-09-17. Per-paper A–K analyses and evidence quotes: `literature/*.md` (six search-stream reports). Novelty matrix and GO/STOP verdict: `NOVELTY_GATE.md`.

## 1. Test-time scaling and ensemble sampling

Repeated stochastic sampling of one model, aggregated by voting, is the simplest inference-time capability amplifier: self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) showed majority voting over diverse chains improves reasoning; "More Agents Is All You Need" (Li et al., arXiv:2402.05120) showed accuracy scales with sample count under voting alone, using a BLEU/frequency similarity that selects the *most* similar sample — the opposite polarity to ours. Recent efficiency work prunes or merges sampled traces using embedding similarity (Slim-SC arXiv:2509.13990; SSDP arXiv:2511.08595; DeepPrune arXiv:2510.08483), but operates within a single agent's trace pool and never constructs inter-agent communication. DIVSE (arXiv:2310.07088) diversifies at the *prompt* level rather than conditioning on responses.

## 2. Multi-agent debate

Du et al. (arXiv:2305.14325, ICML 2024) established multiagent debate: homogeneous instances broadcast answers and critiques over rounds. ReConcile (arXiv:2309.13007, ACL 2024) uses a round-table of *heterogeneous* models with confidence-weighted voting. Subsequent controlled studies complicated the picture: Yang et al. (arXiv:2505.22960) systematically revisited debate as test-time scaling and found conditional effectiveness; Choi, Zhu & Li (arXiv:2508.17536, NeurIPS 2025) compared debate vs. voting with martingale-based theory on fixed graph topologies; Wu et al. (arXiv:2511.07784) ran a controlled Knight–Knave–Spy study questioning whether LLM agents genuinely debate. All of these use broadcast or fixed topologies; none conditions the communication graph on response-level semantic distance.

## 3. Diversity in debate: pruning, retention, initialization

Estornell & Liu (NeurIPS 2024, OpenReview sy7eSEXdPC) is the closest conceptual ancestor: they compute ADA-2 embeddings of responses and pairwise distances, show debate collapses into echo chambers, and prune to a max-diversity *subset* — but the surviving agents still broadcast; there is no pairing, no matching, no reciprocal review. "Diversity of Thought" (arXiv:2410.12853) obtains diversity from different model families. Demystifying MAD (arXiv:2601.19921) initializes debate pools by greedily maximizing the *count of unique answer strings* (no embeddings) and trains confidence with GRPO/LoRA. DAR / "Hear Both Sides" (arXiv:2603.20640) retains the most-disagreeing subset via an LLM filter for broadcast; embedding cosine distance appears only as an evaluation metric, and the paper explicitly "requires no topological changes". S2-MAD (arXiv:2502.04790, NAACL 2025) computes response cosine similarity but with the *opposite* polarity: it suppresses redundant similar viewpoints inside fixed groups. RMoA (ACL 2025 Findings) performs farthest-point-style greedy selection of responses — as inputs to an aggregation mixture, not as review partners. In every case diversity prunes, weights, retains, or initializes; it never builds a one-to-one review topology.

## 4. Distance-conditioned communication topology

CortexDebate (arXiv:2507.03928, ACL 2025 Findings) is the single nearest work: edge weights in its sparse debate graph include output cosine dissimilarity between agents. It differs from FPRR in every structural element: heterogeneous models, dissimilarity as one additive term in a trust formula, a thresholded sparse *broadcast* graph rather than a perfect matching, debate rounds rather than reciprocal pair-limited review, and majority vote rather than synthesis. DySCo (arXiv:2606.01828) lists "cosine distance between reasoning embeddings" as one of five optional edge-scoring signals, with per-receiver top-k senders and one-way critique. DyTopo (arXiv:2602.06039) embeds need/offer *descriptors* (not answers) and routes by similarity — the inverse of farthest pairing. MACE (arXiv:2607.11250) conditions peer selection on response diversity via a LinUCB bandit over n-gram features, explicitly not embeddings. Ha & Mitchell (ICML 2026) use distance purely as an analysis instrument of debate social dynamics.

## 5. Learned and adaptive multi-agent topology

A substantial line optimizes the communication graph itself: GPTSwarm (arXiv:2402.16823, ICML 2024; REINFORCE edge optimization), DyLAN (arXiv:2310.02170; LLM-ranker pruning), AgentPrune (arXiv:2410.02506, ICLR 2025; trained differentiable masks), G-Designer (arXiv:2410.11782; VGAE over profile/query embeddings), MacNet (arXiv:2406.07155; static DAGs, asymmetric instructor→assistant), Graph-GRPO (arXiv:2603.02701; trained GNN policy over role⊕query features, DAG), Guided Topology Diffusion (arXiv:2510.07799, ACL 2026; trained query-conditioned graph diffusion), Codebook Agent (arXiv:2609.02264; trained query-embedding→codebook topology predictor). These condition topology on queries, roles, or learned value estimates — never on the semantic distances among the current responses — and most require training, violating our training-free constraint. Sparse MAD (arXiv:2406.11776, EMNLP 2024 Findings) studies fixed sparse topologies with homogeneous same-prompt agents; it is the closest *structural* relative, but its topology is content-blind.

## 6. Peer review and pairwise critique

Xu et al. (arXiv:2311.08152) implement multi-agent peer review with reciprocal critique, but on an all-to-all graph with full visibility and no distance signal. PiCO (arXiv:2402.01830) pairs agents for mutual assessment but pairs them explicitly at *random* — our C3 control condition, in effect. PRD (arXiv:2307.02762) and FORD (arXiv:2305.11595) use fixed-role or fixed heterogeneous-pair critique. Tournament-style debate (arXiv:2510.05909) pairs adversaries by skill standing, the opposite of epistemic-distance pairing. The scientific peer-review reviewer-assignment lineage (TPMS and successors) matches reviewers to manuscripts by *high* expertise similarity, one-directionally; we found no adaptation of it to reciprocal answer critique among LLM agents.

## 7. Correlated errors and echo chambers

The motivating observation — that same-model samples share assumptions and failure modes, so naive communication reinforces correlated errors — is supported by Estornell & Liu's echo-chamber analysis, Wynn et al.'s failure-mode taxonomy (arXiv:2509.05396), the diversity-scaling analysis of Yang et al. (arXiv:2602.03794), and the 141-study MAD survey (arXiv:2607.26212), which reports 88.7% static topologies and no embedding-distance pairing anywhere in its taxonomy. "Not Birds of a Feather" (arXiv:2607.19785) explores disassortative mixing, but in *personality* space, not response-semantics space.

## 8. Position of this work

FPRR occupies the empty intersection: response-level semantic distance (C, D) → used to construct the communication topology (E) → as farthest one-to-one pairing / perfect matching (F, G) → reciprocal pair-limited review (H, I) → synthesizer aggregation (J) → training-free (K) over homogeneous same-prompt samples (A, B). Each component exists somewhere in the literature; the combination — and the controlled causal question "who should review whom?" asked against random pairing on identical cached responses — does not.
